John goes over development workflow:
- More organisation/coordination between the two teams is needed. Doing peer-review of each other’s work is a good idea.
- DevOps is the practice of software development and integration in a cyclical nature. It is made up of a development phase and a release phase.
- The development phase is made up of planning, coding, building, and testing.
- The release phase is made up of releasing, deploying, operating, and monitoring.
- Integration sits in the middle of these two phases and is critical and often hard.
- DevSecOps is a version of DevOps with security integrated into all phases of the cycle.
- Planning is the most valuable part of the project. A lot of the project’s scope is defined/formalised/expanded here. Good planning leads to good results.
- We will be using scrum, with 2 week sprints:
- The product backlog is a list of everything to be done.
- Items in the backlog need an ID, sizing (in story points), and priority. Planning poker is good for getting a sizing.
- The backlog has a ‘primary’ section with items that can/must be done, a ‘likely’ backlog with items that might be done, and a ‘no’ backlog with things that won’t be able to be completed. This serves as a tool for discussing scope with the client. For example, the client might want to move an item from ‘no’ to ‘primary’. Doing so requires moving stuff of an equivalent size from ‘primary’ to ‘no’. This scheme helps explain to the client the consequences of doing so.
- John discusses communicating benefits over features. A feature is simply what a product is. A benefit is a feature + the reasons that it is good and solves the problem the client has. It puts the feature in context.
